# Field-guide examples

Use these guides to calibrate explanatory depth, professional language, and
editorial selection. All three concern neuroscience or adjacent fields; their
writing choices can inform guides in other disciplines.

## Choose a relevant example

Choose for the editorial question, not only the subject. One guide will usually
provide enough calibration.

| Guide | Particularly useful for |
|---|---|
| [Computational neuroscience](computational-neuroscience-field-guide.md) | Organizing a broad field around models, measurements, computation, and evaluation, with precise terminology and quantitative comparisons. |
| [Systems and cognitive neuroscience](systems-cognitive-neuroscience-field-guide.md) | Connecting cognitive phenomena, learning, population dynamics, and analysis through examples that span levels of explanation. |
| [Neural control](neural-control-field-guide.md) | Connecting disciplinary traditions through states, inputs, observations, objectives, and experiments. |

Borrow the editorial choice, not the syllabus. For example, a materials-science
guide could use the neural-control example's connection between model variables
and measurements without adopting its state-space chapters.

## Compare through passages

Apply the current task, audience, sources, and budget using the
[editing guide](../references/field-guide.md). Compare whether the draft teaches
connected concepts, explains operations through examples, uses precise language,
and retains the most useful foundations and references.

When a quality comparison is requested, point to representative passages in the
draft and example, explain their consequential differences, and recommend concrete
revisions. Judge explanatory value rather than similarity of wording, outline,
chapter count, or length. The examples provide writing calibration, not evidence
for another guide's factual claims.
