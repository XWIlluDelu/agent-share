# Research design and source practice

## What a research prompt should accomplish

Write for a researcher who will return material from which another agent can
learn the field and later edit a concise field guide. Ask for explanations of
actual phenomena, concepts, models, observations, and scientific arguments.
The purpose is durable literacy and research taste, not a catalog of papers,
research questions, fashionable terms, or instructions for a particular project.

Choose directions according to the field. Useful connections include:

- A phenomenon, the distinctions needed to describe it, and representative
  observations or errors that make those distinctions consequential.
- A theoretical construct, its explanatory role, and how competing accounts
  differ in predictions or interpretation.
- A model's variables, assumptions, and operations, connected to the behavior or
  measurements it explains. Give enough detail to understand what it computes.
- A measurement or analytic result, the data object and units behind it, and
  what the estimator or comparison captures.
- Learning, development, history, or changes of scale when these materially
  explain how the field's objects and questions arise.

These are lenses, not mandatory report sections. Select a few productive
connections for each group instead of requiring every topic to cover every lens.
Include relevant methodological foundations without making every report a
methods tutorial. Distinguish mathematical demonstrations or teaching examples
from published empirical results.

## Make each prompt self-contained

Build a natural research brief containing:

- **Purpose and scope:** the group's fields, motivating interests, audience,
  and boundaries; reusable understanding rather than a project deliverable.
- **Substantive directions:** field-specific topics and relationships worth
  learning. Explain why they belong together rather than merely listing labels.
- **Research practice:** read suitable textbooks/reviews and representative
  primary or methods studies; follow sources for important claims; record what
  was actually accessible.
- **Returned artifact:** a coherent Markdown knowledge report with standard
  terminology, concrete explanations, selected empirical/modeling examples,
  sources, and reading-scope notes. Request the complete report, not only a
  teaser or executive summary.

Use headings or a few guiding questions where they clarify the task. Do not
force an identical chapter template, an equal paragraph allocation, or a fixed
paper quota across unrelated groups. Intermediate reports may be richer than
the final guide; their source lists are not the final bibliography.

If earlier work matters, summarize the needed findings or attach the relevant
material in the prompt. Avoid unresolved references such as “follow the first
report” when its content will not accompany the handoff. One group's task should
be executable even when another group's report has not arrived.

In the user's language, ask the researcher to retain authentic technical terms
and explain their use in context. “Research taste” should emerge through the
content: which distinctions are useful, what a strong example clarifies, and
which abstractions preserve the substance of the question. Avoid turning it
into a repetitive audit checklist detached from the science.

## Read sources in either mode

Start with reliable orientation: textbooks, substantial reviews, or comparable
field-appropriate syntheses. Choose primary studies and methods papers that
anchor important claims, explain an influential approach, or reveal a productive
contrast. Include contemporary developments when they change the conceptual
picture; novelty or citation count alone is not a selection principle.

In auto mode, use varied search angles, then retrieve substantive source
content. Inspect the relevant argument, methods, results, figures, or limitations
rather than relying on titles and search snippets. If a source is unavailable,
seek an accessible manuscript or another suitable source; record the remaining
access limitation. Delegated summaries retain the delegate's stated reading
scope and are not personal full-text reads by the coordinating agent.

Keep lightweight notes sufficient to recover:

- What a source contributes and where the supporting material appears.
- Citation identity and a resolvable DOI or stable source link.
- Actual access: full text, relevant sections/figures, abstract only, or a
  secondary account.
- Which statements are observations, theoretical interpretations, or unresolved
  disagreements.

Preserve important uncertainty at its actual location. A disputed interpretation
need not invalidate the underlying phenomenon or every useful result in a field.
Verify consequential conflicts at the source instead of averaging incompatible
claims into bland prose.

The research is sufficient when the group's central concepts and important
connections have substantive support and remaining gaps would not materially
change the proposed guide. Stop unproductive searching; report a genuine access
blocker instead of manufacturing a complete account. Auto execution does not
include running scientific experiments or acquiring new datasets unless the
user separately requests that work.
